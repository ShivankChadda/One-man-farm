# Narration script — The One-Man Farm (animated explainer)

Record each line as its own file named by its ID, e.g. `explainer/vo-recorded/o1.wav`.
Any common format works (wav, m4a, mp3); mono or stereo. Leave a little silence at the start and end — it is trimmed automatically.
Then run `npm run explainer:build`: the animation re-times itself to your recording.

Tips: a quiet room with soft furnishings, phone 20–30 cm from your mouth, a calm, conversational pace.

## Today's farm

- **o1** — This is a farm on Great Nicobar, the southernmost island of India.
- **o2** — Like most farms, it runs on people. Planting, weeding, spraying, picking and carrying. Every day, by hand.

## The idea

- **i1** — Our long-term vision is something we call the One-Man Farm.
- **i2** — It doesn't mean one person does every job. It means that human physical labour largely disappears.
- **i3** — A small team supervises. Machines do the work.

## Water and nutrients

- **w1** — Some of this is already within reach. Drip lines deliver water to every plant, and nutrients travel through the same pipes.
- **w2** — With sensors, automated valves and software, irrigation can run almost on its own.

## Harvesting

- **h1** — Harvesting is much harder.
- **h2** — A machine has to find a ripe vegetable inside dense leaves, reach it without damaging the plant, cut it cleanly, and handle it without bruising.

## Every crop is different

- **h3** — And cucumber, okra, chilli, bitter gourd and papaya may each need a different design.

## Crop protection

- **s1** — Drones have made spraying easier. But on dense, trellised crops, pests hide under the leaves, where spray from above can't reach.
- **s2** — So another machine moves beneath the canopy, spots the pests with a camera, and sprays only where it's needed.

## An ecosystem of machines

- **e1** — Weeding, pruning, training vines, inspecting plants, moving produce. Not one giant machine, but an ecosystem of specialised ones.

## Built for small farms

- **m1** — Most farm machinery was built for vast, flat fields.
- **m2** — Farms here are three, five or ten acres. Irregular, hilly, with narrow tracks and trellises.
- **m3** — So our machines must be small, light and modular. The question is simple: how do we bring industrial productivity to a five-acre farm?

## Data becomes memory

- **d1** — Every machine also collects data. The harvester measures every fruit. The sprayer maps every pest. The irrigation system logs every drop.
- **d2** — Season after season, the farm builds a memory, linking what was done to the crop with what happened to it.

## AI coordinates

- **a1** — On top of that memory, AI can coordinate the whole farm: what is growing where, which plants are struggling, when to harvest, and what to do next.

## Crops for one farm

- **g1** — That data could even shape the seed. Today, crop varieties are bred for broad regions.
- **g2** — In future, a variety could be developed for one farm and its exact conditions, much like personalised medicine.

## The larger opportunity

- **p1** — And if this works on small farms like ours, it can work on millions of others.
- **p2** — Every labour problem becomes an engineering problem. Every solution becomes a product.

## The end state

- **n1** — Machines do the work. Sensors observe. Data remembers. AI coordinates.
- **n2** — That is the One-Man Farm. A farm that keeps learning, built for the realities of smaller farms.
